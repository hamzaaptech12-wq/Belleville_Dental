    Case studies

    Four teaching scenarios

    Composite cases for discussion. They are not real patients.

    Presentation

    A 15-year-old with fixed orthodontic brackets who drinks energy drinks through the day.

    Findings

    White-spot lesions around several brackets and early lesions between back teeth on bitewing X-rays. Heavy plaque along the gumline.

    Management

    Fluoride varnish at each visit, high-fluoride toothpaste as local guidance allows, a plan to limit sugary drinks to mealtimes, brushing coaching around brackets, and a three-month recall.

    Learning points

    Frequency of sugar exposure matters more than the amount. Early lesions can be reversed. Orthodontic patients need extra support.

    Presentation

    A 52-year-old whose blood sugar is above target and who has noticed bleeding when brushing.

    Findings

    Generalised bleeding on probing, pockets of 5 to 6 mm around the molars, and plaque and calculus throughout.

    Management

    Non-surgical periodontal therapy, a home-care plan with interdental cleaning, a letter to the patient's physician, and reassessment after six to eight weeks.

    Learning points

    Diabetes and gum disease affect each other. Treating gum disease may modestly improve blood sugar control, and it improves oral health in its own right.

    Presentation

    A 2-year-old who is put to bed with a bottle of diluted juice.

    Findings

    Chalky and brown lesions on the upper front teeth, with plaque along the gums.

    Management

    Stop sugary drinks at bedtime, brush twice a day with a rice-grain smear of fluoride toothpaste, apply fluoride varnish, and consider silver diamine fluoride to arrest lesions, with the parent warned about dark staining.

    Learning points

    Early childhood caries is preventable. Work with the whole family, and give practical alternatives rather than only instructions.

    Presentation

    A 74-year-old taking several medicines, with a dry mouth and sore teeth near the gums.

    Findings

    Reduced saliva flow, plaque on exposed root surfaces and several soft root cavities.

    Management

    Review medicines with the physician, saliva substitutes and sugar-free gum, high-fluoride toothpaste where prescribed, and a three-month recall.

    Learning points

    Many common medicines reduce saliva. Exposed root surfaces decay more easily than enamel.

    Classroom ideas

    Disclosing plaque

    Use disclosing tablets in pairs, then compare plaque before and after brushing with the timer.

    Sugar diary

    Students log every food and drink for two days and count the sugar exposures, not the grams.

    Explain it to a patient

    Role-play the four case studies, with one student explaining the plan in plain language.
